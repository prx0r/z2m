# Product Portfolio: What to Sell First

## Ranking principles

Score products on:
1. emotional personalization potential;
2. seasonal search intent;
3. AOV;
4. fulfilment simplicity;
5. return/sizing risk;
6. shipping urgency;
7. ability to show a compelling AI transformation;
8. cross-sell fit.

## Tier S — build first

### 1. Christmas ornaments
**Why**
- naturally annual;
- names/dates/pets/families/milestones fit perfectly;
- no sizing;
- collection behavior encourages repeat;
- small/shippable;
- “first Christmas” creates endless recipient niches.

Creative concepts:
- baby's first Christmas;
- first Christmas married;
- first Christmas in new home;
- pet's first Christmas;
- memorial ornament;
- yearly family portrait;
- “our 2026 in one image”;
- hometown/map ornament;
- child’s drawing turned into ornament;
- AI miniature story scene.

### 2. Personalised puzzle
**Why**
- higher AOV;
- obvious before/after visual;
- family/couple/pet fit;
- gift rather than commodity;
- AI can transform ordinary photo into genuinely distinctive art.

### 3. Photo magazine / mini storybook
**Why**
- much harder for Amazon generic merch to commoditize;
- high emotional value;
- uses writing + images + layout;
- can integrate chat history/interview answers;
- premium pricing;
- fantastic gift reveal content.

Products:
- “The 2026 Annual” family magazine;
- “Dad: the unauthorized biography”;
- couple travel mini-book;
- pet magazine;
- child hero adventure;
- “10 things we love about you” book;
- fake museum catalogue.

### 4. Card
**Why**
- repeat occasion;
- huge UK habit;
- easy acquisition;
- reminder loop;
- cheap production.

**But:** use cards to enter the relationship, not as the whole margin pool.

### 5. Calendar
**Why**
- Q4/New Year peak;
- recurring;
- 12 pages give AI lots of creative surface;
- family/pets/travel;
- useful product.

## Tier A — strong attach/cross-sell

### Mug
Easy to understand, strong preview, broad fulfilment.

### Wrapping paper
Excellent attach item: reuse the same recipient visual. Makes an order feel “designed as a set.”

### Gallery board / print
Good margins possible; upsell from generated design.

### Cushion
Giftable and visual, but shipping bulkier.

## Tier B — niche or later

### T-shirts/hoodies
Can work extremely well in *specific* niches:
- family reunion;
- stag/hen;
- sports/fantasy league;
- inside jokes;
- holiday group outfits.

Do not start with generic AI apparel:
- intense competition;
- Amazon directly entered;
- sizing;
- fashion quality expectations.

### Phone cases
Good visual product, but device SKU complexity.

### Generic posters
Cheap and easy but crowded. Need a strong concept niche.

## Hybrid inventory products

For generic seasonal accessories where personalisation is not needed:
- gift bags;
- ribbon;
- ornament hooks;
- boxes;
- stockings/blanks;
- decorative fillers;
- table decor.

Do not dropship these one at a time from China in November.
If validated, import in bulk earlier and place in a local 3PL.

## Best bundles

**£12–£20:** card + ornament  
**£20–£35:** card + mug / print  
**£30–£50:** ornament + puzzle + card  
**£40–£70:** magazine/book + ornament  
**£60–£100:** family memory box / coordinated collection

The bundle is where a low-CAC card customer becomes economically interesting.
