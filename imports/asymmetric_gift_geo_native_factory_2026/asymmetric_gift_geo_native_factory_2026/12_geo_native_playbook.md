# Geo-Native Replication Playbook

## Core thesis
Build **one product engine** and make each market look as though it was created there.

Deployable unit:
`country × language × local occasion × local payment × local proof × local fulfilment`

The backend stays shared: intake schema, generation, calculations, rendering, preview, order state, analytics and fulfilment adapters.

The storefront changes: language, currency, examples, occasions, payment methods, testimonials, delivery promises, legal/support copy.

## Why this beats raw translation
A German buyer should not see “Personalized Christmas gift for Mom — $39.” They should see native German copy, EUR pricing, German examples, local delivery dates and checkout methods. A Dutch buyer should see a Sinterklaas-specific product and iDEAL/Wero. A Norwegian buyer should see NOK and Vipps.

## Tier 1

### Switzerland
Premium digital first. TWINT says it is used by 84% of Swiss online shops. High price tolerance and three languages make one backend unusually leverageable. Start with memoirs, relationship comics, renovation comparison and relocation plans.

### Germany
Best combination of scale and search depth. Destatis/Eurostat reports 85% of German internet users shopped online in 2025. CEWE proves personalized-photo/card behavior and sophisticated Christmas fulfilment. Local ritual wedge: Einschulung/Schultüte.

### Norway
Excellent “small rich language” test. PostNord says Nordic consumers are highly digital; Vipps is most-used and most-preferred in Norway. Native Norwegian copy plus local checkout can outrun generic English stores.

### Netherlands
Best artifact-card lab. Eurostat reports 94% online-shopping penetration. Greetz proves personalized card demand and already offers AI/face-swap/audio/video—so compete with richer artifacts, not generic card creation.

## Tier 2
Sweden: mobile-first, Swish-heavy, highly digital; good for graduation/family/hobby products.

Denmark: 91% annual online shopping; MobilePay preferred; confirmation gifting is a useful local wedge.

France: Fizzer validates personalized cards; attack with crosswords, memoirs, newspapers and story artifacts.

Austria: mostly a marginal-cost extension of the German stack.

## Tier 3
Spain: add Bizum; focus on Navidad/Reyes, communion, weddings and family gifting.

Finland: high readiness but stronger language QA required.

Italy: PhotoSì validates the category, but test organically before larger paid acquisition.

## Country fork method
For every proven English product:
1. Clone canonical landing page.
2. Re-research the exact native query; never literal-translate keywords.
3. Generate local examples.
4. Localize recipient/occasion taxonomy.
5. Add currency and local checkout.
6. Add local shipping cutoffs/POD route.
7. Use native FAQ/support macros.
8. Run a €50–€150 validation test.

Example backend: relationship crossword.
Fork by country, then occasion, then recipient. One engine can support hundreds of landing pages.

## Geo-native moat
The moat becomes:
`localized search history + conversion data + local examples + reviews + payment familiarity + recipient memory + shipping reliability`

The model is shared. The commercial learning compounds locally.
