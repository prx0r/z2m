## Interpretation

### 1. Personalization is already a large paid behavior
Two 2026 market estimates place personalized gifts around $35bn globally. The exact market-size estimate is less important than the direct marketplace evidence: Etsy listings repeatedly show thousands of reviews for simple personalized products.

### 2. Buyers pay for perceived effort, not raw compute
A bespoke song can sell from ~$10 on Etsy to $179+ via Songfinch. Personalized books sell from ~$25 to $70+, and custom comic books can reach triple digits. The input data may only be a questionnaire plus photos.

### 3. Static templates prove pain; personalization is the premium layer
Renovation, wedding, Airbnb and other spreadsheet categories show thousands of reviews. These are primitive products. The opportunity is to ingest the user's *actual* documents/data and return a specific recommendation, comparison or operating pack.

### 4. Low-competition categories can still support premium pricing
Moving-abroad surfaced only ~230 Etsy items, including a $49.95 digital planner. Golf-practice planners surfaced only ~107 items. These are more asymmetric than generic wedding planners or planners/journals.

### 5. Q4 favors emotional legibility
A gift must be understood in one thumbnail and one sentence. “A crossword made entirely from your relationship” is easier to buy than “AI-powered memory synthesis platform.” Marketplace success is driven by occasion + recipient + artifact, not model sophistication.
