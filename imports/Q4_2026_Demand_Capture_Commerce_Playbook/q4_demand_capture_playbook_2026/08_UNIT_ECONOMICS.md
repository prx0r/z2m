# Unit Economics & Cashflow

## Never scale from revenue screenshots

Build a conservative model before launch.

### Variables
- Selling price incl. tax
- Net revenue ex. tax
- Landed product cost
- Fulfillment/shipping
- Payment fees
- Ad CPC
- Conversion rate
- Refund/return %
- Chargeback %
- Customer support cost/order
- Discount cost
- FX / cross-border costs

### Core equations

`CPA = CPC / conversion_rate`

`contribution_before_ads = net_revenue - COGS - shipping - payment_fees - expected_return_cost - support - other_variable_cost`

`break_even_CPA = contribution_before_ads`

`break_even_CPC = break_even_CPA * conversion_rate`

`break_even_ROAS = gross_revenue / break_even_CPA`

## Example

Retail price: £120 incl tax  
Net revenue ex tax (illustrative 20% VAT): £100  
Landed product: £35  
Shipping: £7  
Payment fees: £3  
Expected returns/refunds: £8  
Support/other variable: £2  

Contribution before ads = £45.

At:
- 1% CVR → break-even CPC £0.45
- 2% CVR → break-even CPC £0.90
- 3% CVR → break-even CPC £1.35
- 5% CVR → break-even CPC £2.25

This is why saying “Google CPC is only £1” is meaningless without margin and conversion.

## Working-capital trap

Dropshipping still requires cash:
- ads are paid before payouts settle,
- suppliers need payment immediately,
- refunds arrive after spend,
- payment processors can reserve funds,
- Q4 delivery failures create chargebacks.

Keep a cash reserve. Never spend the full available balance on ads.

## Low-capital target profile

The ideal test product has:
- selling price £70–£250,
- landed gross margin enough to support paid traffic,
- low return rate,
- low support burden,
- light shipping,
- no fragile/battery/medical/IP complexity,
- repeatable supplier stock,
- observable Google search demand,
- a differentiated offer.

These are filters, not guarantees.
