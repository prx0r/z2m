# Localization Arbitrage

## Thesis

The strongest version of “saturated product, unsaturated market” is not random country hopping.

It is:
**proven category + weaker local merchant execution + local-language demand + reliable fulfillment + competitive landed price.**

Google supports localized product feeds, target-country currencies and multi-country setups. In the UK/EEA, Shopping ads operate through Comparison Shopping Services (including Google Shopping and other CSSs).

## Market-screening questions

For each country:
1. Is Google Shopping active and substantial?
2. Is the local language underserved by quality retailers?
3. Can your supplier deliver inside an acceptable window?
4. Can you show local currency and all-in costs?
5. What are consumer-return obligations?
6. What tax/VAT setup applies?
7. How many strong Shopping advertisers exist?
8. Are local incumbents weak at imagery, product data, reviews or assortment?
9. Does the product price leave room for local CPCs?
10. Is there a local-payment expectation you need to support?

## Country expansion pattern

Do not clone 10 stores at once.

1. Prove one category/store in one market.
2. Duplicate feed architecture, not blindly the storefront.
3. Translate manually/AI + human QA.
4. Localize units, currency, delivery, policies and FAQs.
5. Run a small market-specific test.
6. Compare contribution per click, not only ROAS.
7. Keep markets with lower auction pressure and similar conversion.
8. Negotiate local warehouse / faster fulfillment after volume appears.

## UK-specific warning

UK online sellers must present required pre-contract information and delivery details. VAT treatment can apply based on seller/goods location and route to customer. Do not launch paid traffic until your exact setup has been checked.

This playbook is commercial research, not tax/legal advice.
