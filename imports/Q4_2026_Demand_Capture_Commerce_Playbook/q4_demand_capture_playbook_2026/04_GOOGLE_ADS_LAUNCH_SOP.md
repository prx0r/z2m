# Google Shopping / PMax Launch SOP — Low Capital

## Phase 0 — before a single paid click

### Store
- Real business identity and contact information.
- Working email on your own domain.
- Clear shipping page with processing + transit ranges by market.
- Clear returns/refunds with window, conditions, process and who pays return shipping.
- Privacy/terms/contact pages.
- Consistent currency, price and availability.
- No fake countdowns, fake scarcity, invented reviews or false claims.
- Mobile checkout tested.
- At least 5–10 polished hero product pages.
- Realistic delivery date messaging.

### Feed
For every SKU:
- stable ID,
- exact title,
- description,
- price,
- availability,
- link,
- primary image,
- 3+ additional images when possible,
- brand,
- GTIN/MPN when legitimately available,
- Google product category,
- product type hierarchy,
- color/size/material/age/gender where relevant,
- shipping,
- returns,
- custom labels for margin / season / winner tier,
- conversational Q&A attributes where available,
- popularity/ranking attributes where supported.

### Tracking
- Google tag installed.
- Purchase conversion fires once.
- Revenue value and currency correct.
- Test order completed.
- Enhanced conversions if appropriate.
- Analytics event deduplication checked.

## Phase 1 — free traffic sanity test

Enable Merchant Center free listings before paid scale.
Inspect:
- approvals/disapprovals,
- impressions,
- titles,
- image rendering,
- price consistency,
- shipping display,
- product eligibility.

This does not prove paid profitability, but it catches feed/store defects cheaply.

## Phase 2 — controlled paid test

There are two defensible approaches.

### A. Standard Shopping first — maximum control
Best when:
- budget is very small,
- you want clear search-term visibility,
- catalog is small,
- you have zero conversion history.

Use:
- low daily cap,
- manual or conservative bidding,
- product groups by margin,
- aggressive irrelevant-query negatives,
- exact measurement of contribution margin.

### B. Feed-only PMax — faster automation
Best when:
- feed is clean,
- product intent is obvious,
- tracking works,
- enough products exist for the system to learn.

Official Google docs now allow a Shopping-only/feed-only PMax setup by skipping manual creative assets at creation.

Do not call this “set-and-forget.”
Review:
- search terms/insights,
- spend by SKU,
- ROAS vs contribution,
- stock,
- return/refund rate,
- policy issues,
- budget changes,
- brand leakage,
- URL expansion behavior.

## Starting budget rule

Do not copy a guru's $50/day automatically.

Set daily budget from your acceptable learning loss.

Example:
- You can risk £300 total.
- You want 14 days of test runway.
- Daily cap ≈ £21.
- If typical CPC is £0.80, that is ~26 clicks/day.
- If break-even conversion needs 4%, your offer is too fragile.
- If break-even conversion is 1.2%, the test has more room.

## Scaling rule

Do not scale from ROAS alone.
Scale when:
- at least several real orders exist,
- contribution profit is positive,
- refund/return data is tolerable,
- supplier stock is reliable,
- delivery promises are being met,
- query quality is good.

Increase in modest increments (e.g. 10–20%), then observe.
Parallel scaling is often safer:
- add adjacent products,
- expand a winning collection,
- launch another localized market,
- import to Microsoft,
rather than only doubling one campaign's budget.

## Winner isolation

When a SKU gets meaningful spend and strong contribution:
- move it to a priority/winner group,
- build 3–10 adjacent variants,
- create a dedicated buying guide,
- improve images/Q&A,
- negotiate supplier price,
- create a bundle,
- improve delivery,
- add post-purchase cross-sells.

## Kill rules

Pause/rework a product when:
- clicks are high but add-to-cart is near zero,
- conversion requires unrealistic future CVR,
- supplier stock is unstable,
- shopping row contains identical item at a non-defensible price gap,
- returns or support burden erase gross margin,
- misleading claims are needed to sell it,
- shipping misses Q4 cutoff.
