# Operator Playbooks: What Successful / Active People Are Actually Saying

This file distinguishes current operator self-reports from independently verifiable facts.

## Romas Ecom — Blue Ocean / Google demand capture

Observed public thesis:
- use Google rather than only social,
- carry an array of proven products,
- optimize titles/images for Shopping CTR,
- find less-contested search markets,
- capture demand social media helped create.

Useful:
- Search intent arbitrage is valid.
- Product and feed research are systematizable.
- Google Ads Transparency Center can reveal active advertisers.
- The “boring product” framing is useful: evergreen intent often beats novelty.

Do **not** blindly copy:
- revenue/profit screenshots,
- assumed conversion rates,
- simple AliExpress markup,
- giant catalogs before feed/supplier quality is proven.

Most valuable Reddit criticism of the Romas model:
Shopping is a comparison grid. If the same widget is shown by Temu for a fraction of your price, a prettier Shopify theme is not a moat. This is the key adjustment to the entire strategy.

## Arthur & Bryan — Google Ads dropshipping, active 2026

Self-reported scale:
- $500k spend / $1.4m sales over 30 days in one case study.
- Emphasis on feed optimization, product segmentation, negatives, Auction Insights and controlled budget scaling.
- Their 2026 Q&A claims smaller 200–300 product feeds can outperform 1,000+ SKU feeds after a March update.
- They discuss real margins after team/refunds and supplier negotiation.

Recipe to steal:
1. Segment winners from long tail.
2. Optimize titles around high-intent language.
3. Use lifestyle + clean product imagery.
4. Inspect search terms and add negatives.
5. Scale roughly in small increments, not giant budget jumps.
6. Expand horizontally into related SKUs only after a winner proves economics.

## Trevor Fenner / Ecommerce Paradise — high-ticket demand capture

Core play:
- Sell known high-ticket products people already search for.
- Use feed-only PMax / Shopping.
- Optimize feeds and search terms.
- Build product pages for research-heavy buyers.
- Use supplier relationships and branded/product-specific intent.

What transfers to low capital:
- Buyer-intent hierarchy: generic category < brand < exact product/model.
- Match bidding aggression to intent.
- Use custom labels for margin and product priority.
- Treat customer support and trust as conversion infrastructure.

What does not transfer:
- Many high-ticket categories require dealer agreements, serious working capital, damage handling and sophisticated customer service.

## Dropship Breakthru

Most useful reframing:
Google Shopping is not a secret new channel. Serious retailers have used paid search for decades. The edge is executing a retail business better than weak merchants, not “finding a loophole.”

## Reddit operators — repeated signals

Across 2025–2026 threads:
- product/feed titles matching buyer intent matter,
- most tested products do not become winners,
- product pages matter more on Google than many beginners expect,
- higher AOV can help economics,
- scale gradually,
- shipping/returns/price are decisive,
- Q4 rewards work done before October,
- supplier readiness and delivery cutoffs matter,
- Merchant Center inconsistency causes suspensions,
- Google Shopping can work well but is unforgiving of a weak offer.

## Shopify founders — Q4 pattern

From Shopify's founder interviews:
- start promotions earlier,
- inventory and fulfillment planning matter,
- communication matters,
- customer experience matters,
- BFCM is now an extended period rather than only four days.

## Combined operator doctrine

**Demand is not enough. Demand + weak competition + operational credibility + a differentiated offer is the target.**
