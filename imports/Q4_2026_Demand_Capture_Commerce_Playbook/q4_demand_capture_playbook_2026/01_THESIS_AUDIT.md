# Thesis Audit: What Is True, What Is Hype, What Changes in 2026

## Claim-by-claim

| Transcript claim | Verdict | Operational interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Saturated products are validated products | Partly true | Saturation can prove demand, but also proves price competition. Prefer *validated demand with weak merchant execution*, not merely high viral volume. |
| Sell saturated products in unsaturated markets | Strong thesis | Best version is query/market/localization arbitrage. Measure actual Shopping competition, local language quality, seller trust and price spread. |
| Google customers are warmer than social traffic | Generally true for explicit shopping queries | Search intent is stronger, but not all PMax inventory is high intent. Use query controls, feed-only tests, Standard Shopping or AI Max carefully. |
| Google is cheaper | Not universally true | CPC can be cheaper or more expensive. Unit economics determine whether the traffic is useful. |
| No creatives are needed | Technically possible | Feed-only PMax can launch without manual creative assets. But 2026 Google guidance says richer visual assets improve discovery, especially AI shopping. |
| Bigger catalogs always win | False as a rule | More eligible products create more query coverage, but feed quality, supplier accuracy and SKU economics matter more. 2026 operators report success with tighter 200–300 SKU feeds. Low-capital starts should be smaller. |
| 5% conversion is standard | Too aggressive as a planning assumption | Treat conversion rate as unknown until measured. Build break-even models at 1%, 1.5%, 2%, 3% and 5%. |
| 25–35% margins on Google | Possible, not a default | Revenue screenshots omit refunds, payment fees, VAT/tax, chargebacks, returns, support, FX and working-capital costs. |
| PMax is set-and-forget | Wrong | Search terms, feed health, SKU segmentation, budget changes, stock, returns and landing pages require management. |
| Merchant Center approval is easy | Dangerous | Misrepresentation suspensions are widely reported. Policies, feed/site consistency, business identity and shipping accuracy must be correct before launch. |

## The hidden economics problem

A merchant can show:
- 3x ROAS,
- 30% gross product margin,
and still lose money after VAT, payment processing, returns, customer support, chargebacks and currency.

Track contribution profit per order:

`contribution = revenue_ex_tax - COGS - shipping - payment_fees - ad_cost - expected_returns - support_cost - chargebacks - marketplace/CSS fees`

Scale only when contribution remains positive at a conservative return/refund assumption.

## The real moat hierarchy

Weakest → strongest:

1. Same product + higher price.
2. Same product + nicer page.
3. Better title/images/reviews.
4. Local language/currency/policies.
5. Better bundle or configuration.
6. Faster / more reliable fulfillment.
7. Better buying guidance and category expertise.
8. Better warranty/returns/support.
9. Exclusive supplier terms / region availability.
10. Unique bundle, packaging or product variation.
11. Repeat-purchase / community / owned audience.
12. Private label / proprietary product.

The goal is to enter cheaply at levels 3–5 and climb toward 6–10 once a category proves itself.
