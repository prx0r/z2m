# GeoCommerce Premium — amended thesis

## Core thesis
Build one canonical commerce operating system and compile it into **category-coherent consultant stores by country**. The unit of expansion is `category × country`, not `new codebase × country` and not one visible general store containing unrelated products.

The engine looks for proven, evergreen products where:
- the buyer has a real need but is not an expert;
- the buying decision benefits from a 5–15 minute guided consultation;
- gross price is usually ~250–2,500 in local currency equivalent;
- 2,500–5,000 is handled with assisted checkout/human verification;
- above ~5,000 defaults to quote/call/deposit rather than frictionless checkout;
- return risk, regulation, installation and fragility are low enough that contribution survives;
- a reliable supplier can provide verified specs, warranty, inventory and delivery evidence;
- product truth can be turned into premium pages, comparison content, Google Merchant feeds and AI-generated media without inventing claims.

## Why AI changes the geographic game
Historically the expensive part of expansion was not just translation. It was maintaining native-quality product copy, feeds, support, FAQs, media, currency, local search vocabulary and customer service across many markets. Modern LLMs and media models make that marginal cost much lower. The engine therefore exploits geographic distortions: a product family proven in the UK/US/Germany may have meaningful demand but fewer excellent local-language shopping surfaces in Finland, Norway, Belgium, Austria, etc.

## What not to copy from guru playbooks
- Do not duplicate materially identical product listings/stores simply to crowd Shopping positions.
- Do not keyword-stuff claims that are not true.
- Do not let AI alter product geometry or demonstrate capabilities the real SKU does not have.
- Do not use generic China delivery windows for premium positioning.
- Do not treat supplier retail arbitrage as a durable premium supply chain.

## The moat
1. Product/country observation history: demand, bid ranges, benchmark prices, supplier costs, delivery, conversions.
2. Structured product truth: specs, warranty, certifications, GTIN/MPN, logistics, verified media.
3. Market compiler: native search vocabulary + local payment/delivery/tax expectations.
4. Consultant UX: product advisor + human escalation + post-purchase support.
5. Outcome learning: every free-listing impression, click, paid test and sale updates the product/country score.
