# Extensions worth building after the first profitable store

## 1. Supplier reverse-auction
Once the engine observes buyer demand, send anonymized demand forecasts to suppliers: “we have 800 Finnish searches/month and 3.1× CPC headroom; quote landed dropship cost + warranty.” Suppliers compete for the demand you already own.

## 2. Advisor-question moat
Customer chat becomes structured product-research data. If 38% of espresso buyers ask about warm-up time, that field becomes mandatory in supplier ingestion, page comparison and Merchant copy.

## 3. AI-search buying corpus
Publish source-backed “best for X / compare A vs B / what size do I need?” pages from the same product truth. The same structured answer helps human buyers, Google surfaces and AI answer engines without separate GEO gimmicks.

## 4. Cross-country SKU allocator
A SKU need not exist everywhere. Continuously allocate each SKU only to markets where free-listing/paid economics remain attractive. This creates a portfolio rather than a store.

## 5. Dynamic assisted checkout
For $2.5k–$5k purchases, let the advisor build a quote/cart, then route it to a human for a 5-minute verification call. High AOV gets human reassurance without staffing every chat.

## 6. Private-label graduation
Public dropship suppliers are discovery/validation. Winning products graduate to direct supplier terms, branded packaging, local stock or 3PL only after the demand is proven.

## 7. Media asset allocator
Generate several verified demo angles and learn which video/image works per market/search cluster. Media becomes another `asset × country × product` experiment layer.

## 8. B2C-to-prosumer crossover
Some consultant categories naturally reach small businesses: espresso equipment, workshop/storage, office systems, craft equipment. Add VAT invoice, business support and volume bundles without creating a second company.
