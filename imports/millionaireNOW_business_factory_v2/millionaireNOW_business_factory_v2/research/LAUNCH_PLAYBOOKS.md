# Launch playbooks: how to get the first £ without paid ads

## 1) Dormant Database Reactivator — launch first

### Exact offer
"Send me a CSV of old enquiries/quotes/past customers. I will clean it, identify which contacts you can legitimately re-contact, prepare the reactivation sequence, and track booked/held appointments. No ad spend. Pay only for held appointments (or an agreed share of attributable gross profit)."

### Best starting verticals
- roofing / windows / kitchens / bathrooms / remodeling
- HVAC / boiler / heat-pump installers
- high-ticket automotive services
- B2B agencies/consultancies with hundreds of stale leads

Avoid health/financial/legal verticals at first because the communications and data are more sensitive.

### Acquisition
Search Google Maps/LinkedIn for firms already running ads or with mature CRMs. The question is not "do you want AI?" It is "how many unclosed estimates/old enquiries are sitting in your CRM?" Ask for a 100-contact pilot. Use the client's existing authorized sending system initially.

### Suggested economics
- pilot: £0 setup + £30–£75 per held qualified appointment, depending on job value; or
- 10–20% of attributable gross profit for a tightly measured campaign.

Kill if: fewer than ~250 legitimate contacts, low-ticket service, no way to attribute outcomes, or customer cannot establish a lawful contact basis.

## 2) Public Signal Lead Radar — recurring cash engine

### Exact offer
"Every new [project type] application in your patch, filtered to the jobs you actually do, delivered before you would normally find it. One [trade] per territory."

### Best starting wedges
- loft/extension builders by postcode district
- architects targeting larger residential alterations
- roofing/external-envelope contractors where applications imply works
- specialist commercial contractors using procurement/licence/permit signals later

### Pricing anchor
Live competitors currently advertise roughly £39–£149/month depending on geography/service. Enter with a free live feed/sample and sell exclusivity/faster classification rather than a giant dashboard.

### Acquisition
Make public, crawlable evidence pages from genuinely current source records (with source links and observed timestamps), then direct outreach to 20 local contractors per territory. This also creates Google/AI-search inventory without manufacturing generic blog content.

Kill if: signal-to-real-job connection is weak, source data is incomplete in that region, or a territory cannot plausibly create several useful leads/month.

## 3) Tender Qualifier + Bid Desk — niche intelligence, not aggregation

### Exact offer
"I monitor the public contract universe for your exact capability profile. You receive only opportunities that pass your hard gates, plus a one-page bid/no-bid report showing why it fits and what evidence is missing."

### Starting niches
- UK cyber/security consultancies
- MSPs / cloud migration specialists
- accessibility / web-development agencies
- training providers
- specialist facilities/maintenance SMEs

### Pricing
Official search is free and Stotles has a free tier, so do not charge for raw alerts. Test £49–£149/month for tightly qualified niche monitoring or £50–£250 per human-reviewed bid/no-bid brief. Full bid support is a separate higher-ticket service.

### Acquisition
Find suppliers already listed as past awardees in the same CPV/category and firms advertising the capability but with no public-sector content. Send one genuinely relevant current opportunity as the proof.

Kill if: profile is too generic, customer lacks required evidence/accreditation, or fewer than a handful of credible opportunities appear each quarter.

## 4) B2B RFQ Sourcing Desk — manual concierge before marketplace

### Exact offer
"Give me the specification once. I return 3–5 comparable, qualified supplier quotes in one format."

### Starting niches
Good niches have fragmented suppliers, ugly quote processes, high order values and repeat buying:
- custom printed/industrial packaging
- CNC/fabricated low-volume components
- specialist signs/displays/event hardware
- commercial furniture/fit-out components
- specialist print/label runs

### Monetization
Buyer sourcing fee, disclosed supplier referral fee, or negotiated spread. Keep monetization separate from ranking and show it in the UI.

### Acquisition
Recruit supply first by manually listing 10–30 suppliers from one niche and asking whether they accept qualified RFQs. Then create buyer pages around real specification questions and quote ranges only when sourced/observed.

Kill if: suppliers all publish instant commodity pricing, transaction sizes are too small, or buyers require credit/inventory from you before ordering.

## 5) SaaS Renewal Savings Desk — service layer, not software

### Exact offer
"Send your next 5–10 software renewals. I identify seat waste, duplicate categories, renewal risk and sourced pricing evidence. If I negotiate/enable verified first-year savings, I take an agreed success fee."

### Best buyer
20–500 employee firms with meaningful SaaS/AI bills but no procurement specialist. Focus on agencies, SaaS companies, remote companies and AI-heavy teams.

### Competitive reality
SpendHound currently makes its core platform free below 1,000 employees, while enterprise procurement tools charge thousands/month. Therefore a generic dashboard is not competitive. The opening is a founder-operated, performance-aligned service with zero implementation and attention to the few renewals that matter.

### Acquisition
Target companies hiring finance/ops roles, mentioning cost reduction, or visibly using many SaaS products. Give a free arithmetic seat/renewal audit from customer-provided invoices; never pretend you have benchmark data you do not possess.

Kill if: annual addressable spend is tiny, customer cannot share enough contract facts, or savings attribution cannot be agreed upfront.

# Portfolio strategy

If starting broke, do not launch all five brands. Run one service business and use the other kernels internally:

1. Reactivator gets fastest cash.
2. Signal Radar finds prospects/opportunities for the Reactivator and other service customers.
3. Tender Desk becomes a second recurring information product.
4. RFQ Desk tests larger commission transactions.
5. SaaS Savings Desk is an opportunistic high-ROI service when the right buyer appears.

All public pages should expose source links, last-checked timestamps, methodology and machine-readable data where useful. That makes them better for humans, Google and AI search without relying on speculative "GEO hacks."
