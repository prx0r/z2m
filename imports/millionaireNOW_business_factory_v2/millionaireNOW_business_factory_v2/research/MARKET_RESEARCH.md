# Market research — 4 Sep 2026

## 1. SaaS Renewal Savings Desk — strong demand, but differentiate as service

Evidence of spend: Tropic currently lists pricing starting at $3,083/month and says its customer base has delivered $425M in savings, with 21% average customer savings. SpendHound reports 15–20% average SaaS savings and offers a $10,000/year tier for 1,000–5,000 employee firms; its sub-1,000 employee tier is free, proving both demand and severe competition at the software layer.

Implication: do NOT launch another generic SaaS management SaaS. Sell a human/AI-assisted performance-fee renewal desk to smaller firms that will not buy enterprise procurement. The wedge is: "send me your top 10 renewals; I only get paid on verified first-year savings." Start with spreadsheets and customer-provided invoices. Your defensible data asset becomes sourced price observations + negotiation outcomes.

## 2. Public Signal Lead Radar — exceptionally strong zero-capital economics

This is the clearest v2 opportunity. Current UK businesses already sell planning-application alerts to trades for monthly subscriptions: PlanPost advertises from £39/month and says it monitors 389 councils / 2.2M+ applications; PlanLeads advertises £69–£149/month per district; Planning Pipe advertises from £44.95+VAT/month. The UK government Planning Data API exposes planning applications through a consistent API and bulk datasets.

Implication: the market is proven. Don't clone all-UK planning software first. Pick one trade + one geography + one high-value project classifier. Offer the first 20 matching signals free, then exclusive territory. Generalize the same kernel to other public buying signals only after one signal type pays.

## 3. Tender Qualifier + Bid Desk — high value and very proven

UK public bodies spend roughly £385–£400bn/year through procurement. The government explicitly says the Procurement Act regime is intended to open procurement to small businesses. Contracts Finder covers opportunities above £12,000, while Find a Tender covers high-value contracts. Stotles currently charges from roughly £99/month for individual sales intelligence and £495/month for bid tooling, with higher tiers £475–£873+/month.

Implication: data aggregation alone is weak because official sources are free and incumbents already aggregate. The paid wedge is vertical qualification: "Which five of these 200 tenders can *this particular MSP/security consultancy actually win*, and what evidence is missing?" Sell a niche feed + bid/no-bid report + optional bid concierge.

## 4. Dormant Database Reactivator — immediate outcome-based service

The demand is visibly real in 2026: multiple agencies now sell database reactivation at ~$497–$1,497/month, $35–$50/booked appointment, or performance-based structures. InsideSales' large lead-response study found conversion rates were 8x greater in the first five minutes, illustrating how much revenue businesses lose through follow-up failure.

This is superior to cold lead generation for a broke founder because the client already paid to acquire the list. Offer: "give me 500+ old leads; I charge only per held appointment / attributable sale." Begin with email/manual client sending; add SMS/voice only after unit economics cover carrier costs.

Compliance matters. ICO guidance distinguishes corporate subscribers from individuals/sole traders and requires identity/opt-out rules; individual electronic marketing generally requires consent or a valid soft opt-in. The kernel therefore blocks unknown bases and never sends automatically.

## 5. B2B RFQ Sourcing Desk — huge proven behaviour, avoid commodity categories

Alibaba says its RFQ market receives over 20,000 new requests per day across 5,000+ industries and 200+ countries. Thomas says it connects buyers with 500,000+ US suppliers and lets buyers send a quote request to multiple suppliers and compare responses.

Implication: requesting and comparing supplier quotes is extremely proven behaviour. A new generic marketplace is a bad idea. The zero-capital play is a narrow sourcing concierge where specifications are hard and transaction values are high enough to support a referral fee/sourcing fee: custom packaging, fabricated parts, specialist commercial furniture, event hardware, unusual components, etc. Manually recruit 10–30 suppliers and make the buyer experience dramatically easier.

# Ranking for immediate execution

1. **Database Reactivator** — fastest path to cash because client supplies demand inventory and offer can be pay-for-result.
2. **Public Signal Radar** — strongest recurring micro-SaaS; proven £39–£149/month pricing and free/public upstream data.
3. **Tender Bid Desk** — very large money pool and high willingness to pay, but trust/vertical expertise matters.
4. **RFQ Sourcing Desk** — potentially huge commissions, but requires supplier recruitment and quote operations.
5. **SaaS Savings Desk** — hard-dollar ROI, but software layer is fiercely competitive; position as performance service, not SaaS.

# One-template doctrine

Each app is a kernel, not a final brand. New verticals should change config/data/offer rather than infrastructure. Reject a vertical if it requires paid acquisition, inventory, bespoke code, or an external integration before the first customer can receive value.
