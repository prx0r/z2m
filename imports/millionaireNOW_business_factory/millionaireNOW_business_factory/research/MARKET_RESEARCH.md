# 1. Micro quote / lead marketplace

## Why the market is real

Google Local Services Ads are themselves a pay-per-lead market. SearchLight Digital's February 2026 dataset covers $6.72m of spend across 888 home-service contractors and reports a blended $53 lead, with category figures including electrical $39, HVAC $51, plumbing $57 and water heaters $71. It also reports a 43.9% book rate, $1,826 average ticket and 7.84x closed ROAS in the observed data.

Shared-lead aggregators are also an established category. Current contractor comparisons describe shared leads commonly being sold to multiple contractors for roughly $10–$100 depending on vertical and lead type.

## Why a tiny entrant can compete

Do not compete with Angi/Thumbtack/Bark on breadth. Compete on **one exact purchase event**:

- “New-build snagging quote in Manchester”
- “Commercial office cleaning in Bristol, 5k–25k sq ft”
- “Independent home EV charger installation quote”
- “Damp survey for pre-purchase buyers”

The advantage is richer qualification and exclusivity. A provider would rather receive one enquiry that states job type, geography, budget, urgency, property attributes and consent than five generic shared form fills.

## Best starter verticals

### A. Home inspection / new-build snagging
Why: meaningful ticket, location-based, finite questionnaire, lower emergency burden than plumbing, relatively easy provider onboarding.

### B. Commercial cleaning
Why: recurring contract value, buyers can specify building size/frequency/location, suppliers value qualified decision-maker enquiries. B2B also avoids some consumer-service complexity.

### C. Specialist removals
Examples: piano moves, office relocations, international student moves. High information asymmetry and quotable requirements.

### D. Solar / battery installation
Excellent lead economics but substantially more competitive; financing, claims, installer certifications and changing regulations make this a second-wave niche.

### E. MSP / outsourced IT
Very high customer lifetime value; use a “requirements matcher” rather than a local-trades aesthetic.

## Revenue model

Start with manual supplier contracts. Charge only for an **accepted qualified lead**, or better, a **held appointment**. Do not build payments infrastructure before providers agree to pay.

Expected-value ceiling:

`max lead price = lead-to-sale probability × provider gross profit per sale × acquisition share provider will tolerate`

The app's starter heuristic must be replaced by outcome data.

# 2. Premium advisor / affiliate -> dropship

## Why this is stronger than commodity dropshipping

Shopify's current guidance says typical open-market dropshipping margins are often around 10–15%, while supplier-network relationships can be materially higher; its 2026 product guidance cites broad dropshipping margins of roughly 10–40% depending on niche and execution. High-ticket dropshipping removes inventory but increases trust, returns, support and delivery risk.

The stronger zero-capital launch is therefore **affiliate/referral first**. Prove that the advisor can produce sales, then negotiate dealer pricing for winning SKUs.

## Concrete current premium affiliate economics

- SaunaCloud advertises 7% commission, an average sale above $17,000, and typical payout above $1,200 per sale.
- Topture advertises up to 6% on sauna orders roughly $3,000–$12,000.
- Sauna V advertises 10% commission on eligible saunas.
- Massage Chair Heaven advertises 5% commission on qualified referrals/purchases.
- The Standing Desk's Awin profile advertises up to 10% commission.
- Semrush's affiliate program currently advertises $100–$300 per sale plus $10 trials, illustrating similar hard-dollar economics in B2B software.

## Best product characteristics

Choose products where:
1. AOV is high enough that one conversion matters.
2. Buyers open many tabs because specification/fit is confusing.
3. Multiple suppliers/affiliate programs exist.
4. Shipping/returns are not catastrophically frequent.
5. Buyers care about warranty, lead time, dimensions, compatibility or use case.
6. Price/spec data changes enough that verified freshness is useful.

## Best categories to test

### A. Home saunas — strongest researched example
High AOV, several direct affiliate/dealer programs, complex fit questions (space, indoor/outdoor, capacity, traditional/infrared, electrical/install constraints), and buyers need trust.

### B. Massage chairs
High-ticket, comparison-heavy, current direct affiliate programs. Strong advisor fit around user height, space, warranty, features and service coverage. Avoid medical claims.

### C. Premium standing desks / ergonomic office
Lower ticket but easier logistics and several affiliate routes. Strong content/SEO surface.

### D. Portable power stations / home backup
Specification-heavy and well suited to a calculator/advisor. Avoid making electrical-safety or installation claims beyond manufacturer documentation.

### E. Premium coffee / espresso setups
Configuration and bundle opportunity. Better as advisor + affiliate initially than generic storefront.

## Why verified pricing matters

The real differentiator is not “AI chatbot”. It is: **this recommendation used prices/specs checked at X time from Y source**. That is the LiveLLM pattern applied to physical commerce.

# 3. AI/search site audit -> paid fix

## Is the AI-discovery pitch real?

Yes, but phrase it carefully.

OpenAI says public websites can appear in ChatGPT search and advises publishers not to block OAI-SearchBot if they want content discoverable. Google says its normal SEO fundamentals remain applicable to AI Overviews/AI Mode and explicitly says there is no special AI text file or special schema required.

Adobe's April 2026 data, based on more than one trillion U.S. retail visits, reported AI-source traffic to retail sites up 393% YoY in Q1 2026 and March AI referrals converting 42% better than non-AI traffic. However, Semrush's analysis across 50,000+ sites and 17 industries found AI traffic still under 0.15% of total visits in its sample even while growing quickly.

**Conclusion:** “AI visibility” is a strong emerging pain and sales hook. It is not honest to tell the average small business that most of its traffic is AI.

## What the free audit should measure

- OAI-SearchBot / Google crawl access
- important facts visible in server-delivered HTML
- title, headings, canonical/entity clarity
- structured data that matches visible content
- current product/service prices where appropriate
- contact/location/service-area clarity
- policy/trust pages
- conversion path and CTA
- sitemap/internal-link discoverability
- freshness dates and source provenance

## Monetization

`free URL audit -> specific evidence-backed fixes -> fixed-price implementation -> monthly regression monitor`

Do not sell the score. Sell the fixes.

## Best verticals

1. Local service businesses with expensive leads: roofers, HVAC, inspections, clinics, solicitors, MSPs.
2. Ecommerce with product data: premium stores, specialist retailers.
3. B2B SaaS with comparison/search demand.
4. Multi-location businesses where entity/location clarity matters.

# 4. Verified current comparison engine

## Demand proof

Affiliate marketing is a mature performance channel. impact.com's 2025 global survey reports 74% of surveyed brands generated 11–30% of company revenue from affiliate marketing. Shopify's 2026 affiliate-network guidance lists large active publisher ecosystems and its own program pays up to $150 per qualified referral in some locations. Semrush currently advertises $100–$300 per affiliate sale.

## Why generic comparison is hard

“Best CRM 2026” and similar keywords are dominated by large publishers. A new site should not attempt broad listicle SEO.

## Where the opening is

Build around **facts that go stale and a narrow buyer**:

- cheapest API/provider for a defined workload
- UK accounting tools for a two-person trade business
- ergonomic desk under a specific dimension
- sauna that fits a 1.5m × 1.5m indoor room
- MSP quotes for 20–50 person London professional firms

Every page exposes source URLs, last-check time and ranking logic.

## AI/search discoverability

Google says product snippet structured data is appropriate for editorial/aggregator product pages; merchant-listing schema is for pages where the merchant actually sells the item. The code therefore treats comparison pages as editorial/aggregate data rather than pretending to be the merchant.

# 5. Vertical outbound / appointment setting

## Demand proof

Current UK B2B appointment-setting guides commonly quote roughly £250–£600 per booked meeting or multi-thousand-pound monthly retainers; other 2026 guides report a wider $250–$1,500+ per-meeting market depending on qualification and deal value.

That is clear evidence that **a held qualified conversation is a saleable unit**.

## Why not launch this as “AI outreach”

The market is saturated and easy to copy. Use the engine first to acquire suppliers and customers for the other four businesses. Only sell it once one vertical's process is proven.

## Competitive version

Not: “we get B2B meetings”.

Instead: “We book held meetings between 20–100 employee professional firms and MSPs that fit an agreed stack/region/budget profile.”

## Compliance design

The supplied kernel creates drafts and requires approval; it does not mass-send. In the UK, ICO guidance distinguishes corporate subscribers from sole traders/individual subscribers under PECR. Corporate B2B email can have different consent rules, but identity, opt-out, data-protection lawful basis and suppression handling still matter. Build jurisdiction-specific compliance before automating volume.

# Where AI-search optimization actually creates a moat

The emerging opportunity is **not GEO snake oil**. It is making a site the clearest current source of a narrow answer.

A strong page should contain:
- one precise question/purchase intent
- current factual answer high on the page
- visible methodology
- source links
- last-updated/check timestamps
- server-visible text
- coherent entities/products/services
- structured data that matches the visible content
- stable canonical URLs
- machine-readable API/feed where useful

Google's own AI-features guidance says normal SEO fundamentals still apply and that no new AI-only markup is required. OpenAI says OAI-SearchBot access is a prerequisite for discoverability eligibility, not a ranking guarantee.

# Recommended first 30-day portfolio

## Test 1 — premium affiliate advisor: home sauna
- one domain
- 20–50 products/offers from approved partners
- advisor questionnaire
- “best for X” pages generated from real dimensions/price/warranty data
- source + checked-at on every fact
- organic/community acquisition only initially
- success metric: qualified outbound clicks / quote requests

## Test 2 — micro quote market: home inspection or commercial cleaning
- onboard 5 providers before chasing traffic
- agree exact accepted-lead definition and price
- launch 10–30 high-intent location/service pages with genuinely distinct facts
- success metric: accepted lead value per visitor

## Test 3 — AI/search audit funnel
- use outbound kernel to identify 50 relevant businesses
- run public audit
- offer the specific fixes, not a vague “GEO package”
- success metric: audits -> replies -> paid fixes

After 30 days, kill anything without a measurable transaction signal. Fold winning data into Verified Compare.

# Risks that matter

## Dropshipping/direct selling
If you are seller of record, the supplier does not magically absorb your consumer obligations. Returns, refunds, delivery promises, product safety, tax/VAT and chargebacks can sit with you depending on structure/jurisdiction. Affiliate mode removes much of this operational risk.

## Thin SEO sites
Do not generate hundreds of near-identical doorway pages. The reusable template is infrastructure, not an excuse for duplicate content. Each vertical/location page needs actual useful data.

## Rankings and affiliate bias
Do not let commission determine factual ranking. If a placement is sponsored, label it.

## Fake freshness
Never write “checked today” unless the source was actually checked today. Stale data should remain visible as stale rather than silently refreshed with a timestamp.

## Lead consent
If an enquiry is going to be shared with third parties, make that clear at collection. Keep an audit log of consent and supplier routing.
