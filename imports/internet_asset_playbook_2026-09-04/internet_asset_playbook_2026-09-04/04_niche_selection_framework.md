# 4. Niche selection framework

Score each candidate from 0–5 on seven dimensions.

## A. Repeated intent

Does the user repeatedly need this information, or is it one-off trivia?

Good:

- “find an installer”
- “compare providers”
- “what does this cost now”
- “which jobs are open”
- “which vendors meet X requirement”

Weak:

- definitions;
- celebrity/news summaries;
- generic how-to questions AI can answer instantly.

## B. Economic value of the user

Would a supplier pay £20–£500+ to acquire this person/company?

The best niches involve an imminent commercial action.

## C. Fragmentation

Is the information scattered across dozens or hundreds of websites, PDFs, forums, social posts, and vendor pages?

Fragmentation creates the opportunity for aggregation.

## D. Freshness / data moat

Does the information change?

Changing data is good because:

- users return;
- stale incumbents become weak;
- automated monitoring creates real value;
- AI summaries cannot rely on a timeless generic answer.

## E. Monetization depth

Require at least three plausible paths, such as:

- supplier sponsorship;
- affiliate program;
- lead fee;
- paid alert;
- premium listing;
- data subscription;
- transaction commission.

## F. Distribution wedge

Can you name at least one path to the first 500 users that is not “wait for Google”?

Examples:

- a subreddit with recurring requests;
- X community;
- professional Slack/Discord;
- vendor outreach;
- newsletter;
- free calculator;
- downloadable report;
- existing domain/project audience.

## G. Defensibility

Can the dataset become harder to reproduce over time?

Strong moats:

- verification;
- historical data;
- user submissions;
- private partner feeds;
- manually checked records;
- transaction reviews;
- response-time data;
- price history;
- unique scoring methodology.

## Score thresholds

- **29–35:** build/validate now.
- **24–28:** promising; test monetization before scaling.
- **18–23:** only build if you have a distribution edge.
- **<18:** likely a content-farm trap.

## Hard rejection rules

Reject a niche if any of these are true:

1. The best page would contain mostly prose that an LLM can answer directly.
2. You cannot identify three realistic monetization buyers/programs.
3. There is no legal/allowed way to source enough data.
4. Every page would differ only by a keyword/location token.
5. The site needs hundreds of pages before a single page is useful.
6. You would need fake reviews, invented prices, or unsourced claims.
7. The niche is YMYL/high-stakes and you cannot provide expert verification.

## Pre-build validation test

Before writing the app, produce this evidence packet:

- 20 real search/query examples;
- 50 candidate entities/records;
- 10 recurring complaints/questions;
- 10 potential paying vendors;
- 3 monetization programs or buyer types;
- 1 distribution channel;
- 1 manually created “perfect page”;
- 1 pre-sale or explicit vendor interest signal.

If you cannot do this manually, automation will not rescue the niche.
