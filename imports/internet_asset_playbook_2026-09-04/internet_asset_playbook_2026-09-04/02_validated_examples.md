# 2. Validated examples

## Evidence grading

- **A — payment-provider verified:** third party shows revenue linked to Stripe/RevenueCat/Polar/etc.
- **B — strong first-party:** founder or company gives detailed revenue figures, usually with long-running public history.
- **C — self-reported case study:** plausible but not independently verified.
- **D — marketing claim:** no underlying financial evidence located.

## 1) JobBoardSearch — the closest clean proof of the thesis

**Model:** meta-directory of niche job boards.

**Current evidence:** TrustMRR showed **$2,215 revenue in the last 30 days** and **$96,466 all-time revenue**, verified via Stripe, last updated 31 Aug 2026.

**Earlier founder economics:** the founder has publicly described sponsored slots, listing upgrades, ad/CPC revenue, and later SEO/link-building services. An earlier Indie Hackers update reported $4,887 in a single month and $50K cumulative revenue.

**Why it matters:** this started as a very small, simple directory. The asset became valuable because it accumulated audience, search rank, community, and a list of buyers who themselves wanted visibility.

**Evidence grade:** A.

**Copyable lesson:** build a free utility for one side of a market; charge the economically motivated side for placement, access, or leads.

---

## 2) TrustMRR — a directory becomes a data product and marketplace

**Model:** database of startups with payment-provider-verified revenue, plus acquisition marketplace.

**Current evidence:** TrustMRR showed **$39,776 revenue in the last 30 days**, **$19,036 MRR**, and **$317,592 all-time revenue**, verified via Stripe on 4 Sep 2026.

**Monetization:** current terms list paid marketplace tiers ($29/$199/$499), authority/AI-visibility add-ons, sponsor placements, a paid signals database, buyer alerts, and a **3% marketplace closing fee**.

**Why it matters:** this is the stronger evolution of the directory thesis. The differentiator is not prose; it is verified structured data. The directory then creates secondary businesses: subscriptions, sponsorship, premium placement, and brokerage.

**Evidence grade:** A.

**Copyable lesson:** verification itself can be the moat. If your records are more trustworthy than generic search results, the database can support several revenue streams.

---

## 3) AffiliList — tiny directory, small but real passive-ish revenue

**Model:** affiliate-program directory.

**Current evidence:** TrustMRR showed **$117 revenue in the last 30 days** and **$2,072 all-time revenue**, Stripe-verified as of 3 Sep 2026.

**Pricing:** $39 per submission.

**Why it matters:** this is much closer to the small-scale promise in the post. It does not prove huge upside, but it proves a narrow directory can collect money for listings with very limited product complexity.

**Evidence grade:** A.

**Copyable lesson:** the simplest monetization can be “pay to be included / reviewed / highlighted,” provided the audience is useful to the lister.

---

## 4) DirectoryHunt / StartupDirectory.net — proof that “make a directory” is not enough

**DirectoryHunt:** roughly **$80 all-time revenue**, Stripe-verified, 3 Sep 2026.

**StartupDirectory.net:** roughly **$120 all-time revenue**, payment-provider-verified, though its API connection had gone stale by April 2026.

**Why these matter:** they are the necessary counterexample to the viral framing. A directory boilerplate plus SEO wiring does not create demand, differentiation, or buyers automatically.

**Evidence grade:** A for the captured payment data; currentness varies.

**Copyable lesson:** your niche, dataset, and distribution matter more than the directory software.

---

## 5) Real Work From Anywhere — automated aggregation + ads + paid listings

**Model:** remote-job aggregation, originally using feeds and later a scraper/alert stack.

**Founder report:** in April 2025 the founder said the site had averaged above **$5K revenue per month for three months**, had made **$16,439 since the start of 2025**, and cost roughly **$6 for hosting**. The first paid job listing arrived in March 2025; much of the earlier revenue was advertising.

**Evidence grade:** C (detailed self-report; not payment-provider verified in the source used).

**Important lesson:** the project reportedly made essentially nothing for a long time. The revenue was not immediate. Distribution and accumulated traffic were the asset.

---

## 6) Nomad List + Remote OK — mature “boring internet assets” at serious scale

**Model:** structured destination database/community plus remote-job board.

**Founder history:** Pieter Levels reported the pair exceeding **$1M annualized revenue** in 2019, with 11,996 customers and no paid advertising. A later first-party interview with Stripe’s John Collison referenced Nomad List reaching about **$700K ARR** and Remote OK generating about **$3.4M in revenue**.

**Evidence grade:** B.

**Why it matters:** these are archetypal internet assets: searchable structured data, community, jobs, memberships, and sponsorship. They also demonstrate that a free data layer can be the acquisition engine for paid side products.

---

## 7) Tooltester — comparison/review search traffic monetized via affiliate economics

**Model:** deeply researched software reviews and comparisons.

**Reported economics:** Starter Story’s 2026 profile says Tooltester generates about **$120K/month** and began with roughly **$200** in starting cost. Team size is listed as 9.

**Evidence grade:** C/B- (founder case-study ecosystem; not current Stripe verification).

**Why it matters:** affiliate sites still work when they provide research, testing, structured comparisons, and commercial-intent pages. This is very different from generic AI-written “best X” pages.

---

## 8) Programmatic SEO at large companies — proof of the page-family mechanism

Zapier, Wise, Canva, G2, Tripadvisor, and similar businesses use templated page families successfully. The important pattern is that pages are backed by **unique structured data or tools**: integrations, live exchange rates, templates, reviews, salaries, local inventories, etc.

This validates programmatic publishing as an engineering technique, not mass AI prose as a business model.

**Evidence grade:** B for observable site architecture; traffic estimates are third-party and directional.

---

## 9) The Lummox / Grok Bot $217 example

**Model claimed:** bot finds niche, builds directory, publishes nightly, conducts affiliate outreach, sends revenue reports.

**What I could validate:** the post exists in mirrors; GrokBot has a public “Internet Real Estate Research and Outreach” run showing complaint research, page drafting, scheduled publishing, affiliate outreach preparation, and reporting workflows.

**What I could not validate:** the named site behind the $217 month-three figure, its analytics, payment records, or affiliate dashboard.

**Evidence grade:** D for the revenue claim; B for the existence of the workflow demonstration.

## What the examples collectively say

1. **Yes, small internet assets make money.**
2. **Many make almost nothing.**
3. **The winners usually have a data, utility, or distribution moat.**
4. **The highest-value monetization is often not display ads.**
5. **The “passive” state generally comes after a long period of compounding, not before.**
