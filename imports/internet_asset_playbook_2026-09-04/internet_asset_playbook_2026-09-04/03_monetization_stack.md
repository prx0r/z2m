# 3. Monetization stack

## Ranked by value per useful visitor

### Tier 1 — direct leads / introductions

**Best for:** expensive services, B2B, installers, consultants, commercial equipment, finance-adjacent providers where compliant.

Mechanisms:

- pay per qualified lead;
- pay per booked call;
- referral fee on closed sale;
- flat monthly territory sponsorship;
- managed quote request.

Why this is strong: 100 valuable visitors can be worth more than 100,000 generic ad-supported pageviews.

Example model:

- 10 suppliers at £99/mo = £990
- 20 qualified leads at £40 = £800
- total = **£1,790/mo** before affiliate or display revenue

This is a scenario, not a benchmark.

### Tier 2 — premium listings / sponsorship

**Best for:** directories where vendors care about being discovered.

Products:

- featured listing;
- category sponsor;
- newsletter sponsor;
- regional sponsor;
- verified badge;
- enhanced profile;
- inclusion in comparison tables;
- lead alerts.

This is JobBoardSearch’s core pattern and part of TrustMRR’s current model.

### Tier 3 — affiliate / referral commissions

**Best for:** software, hosting, ecommerce tools, travel, business services, products with trackable online conversion.

Current official examples show why software can be attractive:

- Semrush: current program advertises roughly $100–$300 per sale depending on toolkit, plus some trial bounties.
- Kinsta: $50–$500 one-time plus 10% recurring for eligible WordPress-hosting referrals.
- Webflow: 50% revenue share on a qualifying new user’s first eligible subscription for up to 12 months.

The implication: a tiny number of high-intent conversions can fund a niche site.

### Tier 4 — subscription / alerts / data access

**Best for:** changing datasets.

Examples:

- new tenders;
- price drops;
- job alerts;
- startup signals;
- supplier availability;
- regulation changes;
- investment/subnet/operator metrics;
- expiring grants;
- new vendor launches.

Recurring revenue is stronger than one-time page traffic because the user pays for continuing freshness.

### Tier 5 — marketplace take rate / brokerage

**Best for:** high-value transactions where buyers and sellers need discovery + trust.

TrustMRR currently charges a 3% closing fee on marketplace transactions, showing how a directory/database can graduate into brokerage.

### Tier 6 — productized service attached to the asset

A directory can generate demand for a service:

- “we shortlist three suppliers for you”;
- “we run the security assessment”;
- “we configure the stack”;
- “we produce your comparison matrix”;
- “we submit your listing to 50 directories”;
- “we verify your profile/data.”

This is often the fastest path to cash when traffic is still small.

### Tier 7 — display ads

Use only when traffic is large and commercial alternatives are weak. Ads are easy, but usually the lowest-value monetization per visitor.

## Monetization sequence I recommend

Do not wait for traffic before testing monetization.

1. Pick a niche with identifiable suppliers/buyers.
2. Create 10–30 excellent free records/pages.
3. Ask 20 vendors what one qualified lead or premium placement is worth.
4. Pre-sell one sponsor / premium listing / paid research request.
5. Add affiliate programs only where they genuinely match user intent.
6. Add subscription/alerts once freshness becomes useful.
7. Add ads only after the higher-value paths are exhausted.

## The strongest structural model

**Free data -> paid visibility -> paid intent -> transaction fee -> premium intelligence.**

That ladder is much more robust than “SEO traffic -> AdSense.”
