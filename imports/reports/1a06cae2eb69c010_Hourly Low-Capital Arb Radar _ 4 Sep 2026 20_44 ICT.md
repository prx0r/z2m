## 1. Senior-living placement concierge — 9.5/10
Money flow: family uses your free advisor to find assisted living/memory care; you qualify needs, shortlist facilities, arrange tours; participating community pays after move-in. Haven says it charges one month of base rent after move-in; Senioridy cites ~70–80% of first-month rent, often $3k–$5k. Startup estimate £50–£300, almost no working capital. Fulfillment: licensed local communities; you need referral agreements before sending prospects. AI edge: empathetic intake, budget/care-needs normalization, facility comparison, availability collection, tour scheduling, document checklist—without pretending to make medical judgments. Google Ads: `[assisted living near me]`, `[memory care + city]`, `[senior living advisor]`; negatives: jobs, careers, free housing, nursing degree. Landing page: “Tell us what Mum/Dad needs; get a verified shortlist.” Best geography: private-pay US metros. Risks: state referral-disclosure/licensing rules, vulnerable consumers, conflicts of interest; disclose compensation clearly. 48h test: build one-city landing page and sign 5 communities before spending £50. Moat: real availability/pricing database + family intent data + trusted facility network. Domains: CareMatchLocal, HavenGuide, SeniorShortlist.

Evidence: Haven for Seniors; Senioridy.

## 2. UK commercial-finance introducer — 9.4/10
Money flow: SME asks for funding; your AI gathers turnover, purpose, term, security and documents; regulated/authorized broker completes advice/placement; you receive an introducer share. Tera currently advertises zero fees and 30% of its net commission on completed deals; its example shows £1,500 to the introducer when Tera receives £5,000. Startup £0–£150; no lending capital. AI edge: instant funding-fit questionnaire, document readiness, routing by invoice finance/asset finance/bridging/commercial mortgage. Ads: `[business loan £100k]`, `[asset finance machinery]`, `[invoice finance UK]`; negatives: personal, payday, student, bad-credit consumer terms unless partner supports them. Best geography: UK SMEs. Risks: FCA perimeter, financial-promotion rules, consent/privacy; stay introducer, not unlicensed adviser. 48h: get accepted by 2 broker partner programs; create one niche page for “finance for dental practices” or “finance for workshops”; spend £30–£50 only after agreements. Moat: proprietary lender-fit and completion-rate data by SME type. Domains: CapitalRoute, TradeFunding, FundFit.

Evidence: Tera Business Finance; The Business Finance Network; Clear Idea Finance.

## 3. Luxury-villa AI trip concierge — 9.2/10
Money flow: affluent traveler describes dates, party, destination, staff/amenities; AI shortlists partner inventory; villa operator handles property/booking; you earn travel-partner commission. Privadia states 10% standard commission and offers unbranded links, live availability and trade dashboard. Oliver’s Travels says affiliates average £180 per completed booking; higher-end advisor channels can be much larger. Startup £50–£300; customer usually pays operator, so little working capital. AI edge: “find a staffed 6-bedroom Ibiza villa, walkable to beach, child-safe, under €18k/week,” then coordinate chef/boat/transfer upsells. Ads: `[luxury villa Ibiza]`, `[private villa St Barts]`, `[staffed villa Tuscany]`; negatives: cheap, hostel, long-term rent. Best geography: UK/US outbound to Mediterranean/Caribbean. Risks: travel-agent/package-travel rules depending how you bundle; begin as referral/affiliate rather than merchant-of-record. 48h: join 2 programs, launch one destination microsite, manually answer 20 long-tail requests. Moat: preference profiles + destination inventory normalization + repeat affluent clients. Domains: VillaBrief, PrivateStay, EstateSelect.

Evidence: Privadia; Oliver’s Travels; Villas of Distinction advisor programs.

## 4. Used heavy-machinery brokerage — 9.1/10
Money flow: owner consigns excavator/tractor/truck; you market and qualify buyers; buyer/seller settle through agreed process; you collect success fee. Ironmart’s 2026 brokerage guide cites roughly 8–15% on excavators/dozers, 5–10% on heavy trucks/trailers and 10–18% on agricultural equipment, varying with value and complexity. Startup £50–£300; no inventory if brokerage/consignment only. AI edge: normalize specs/hours/attachments, produce valuation comps, multilingual listings, buyer matching, inspection checklist, financing introductions. Ads: `[used 20 ton excavator for sale]`, `[used CNC machine UK]`, model-number searches; negatives: toys, manuals, parts unless monetized. Best geography: UK/EU initially; expand cross-border with logistics partners. Risks: title/ownership, liens, fraud, export controls, VAT, condition misrepresentation. 48h: select one machine class, identify 100 stale listings, offer owners success-fee-only representation, build searchable mini-catalog. Moat: verified seller/buyer graph + pricing history. Domains: MachineMatch, IronDesk, PlantSelect.

Evidence: Ironmart Online 2026 commission guide.

## 5. Business broadband / VoIP reseller — 9.0/10
Money flow: business enters postcode, seats, usage and resilience needs; you recommend a carrier package; authorized partner provisions it; you earn commission. Netify currently advertises up to 21% upfront on BT business broadband, up to 22.4% on Cloud Voice and up to 35% of first-year charges on certain Ethernet/SD-WAN products; Wavetel advertises 10% recurring commission for affiliate partners. Startup £0–£150; no network infrastructure. AI edge: translate telecom jargon into a two-minute recommendation, compare failover/static IP/voice/contract economics, collect bills for switching. Ads: `[business leased line quote]`, `[business broadband postcode]`, `[replace office phone system]`; negatives: home broadband, consumer mobile, speed test, support. Best geography: UK. Risks: telecom sales compliance, contract disclosures, service complaints. 48h: join one reseller route, build “business connectivity check” for one city/industry. Moat: renewal dates + customer estate data + recurring commissions. Domains: OfficeLine, FibreDesk, BusinessLinkup.

Evidence: Netify BT reseller program; Wavetel Business.

## 6. Yacht-charter retail broker — 8.9/10
Money flow: client specifies destination, dates, group and preferences; central charter agent/owner provides yacht and crew; retail broker receives a share of embedded charter commission. Industry sources describe ~15% gross MYBA-style charter commission, commonly split between central and retail sides; Ahoy Club cites 15–20% industry convention. Startup £100–£500 for credible brand/CRM; no yacht ownership. AI edge: itinerary/yacht matching, APA/fuel scenario explanation, cabin/crew constraints, rapid quote comparison. Ads: `[luxury yacht charter Croatia]`, `[superyacht charter Greece]`; negatives: jobs, crew, cheap boat rental, license course. Geography: UK/US/UAE clients booking Med/Caribbean. Risks: charter contracts, KYC/AML, client-money handling—prefer partner structure where established broker handles funds. 48h: partner with charter house, build one destination advisor and test organic/outbound before paid ads. Moat: repeat UHNW customer profiles + preference history. Domains: CharterBrief, YachtSelect, PrivateVoyage.

Evidence: Ahoy Club; YachtsForKings.
