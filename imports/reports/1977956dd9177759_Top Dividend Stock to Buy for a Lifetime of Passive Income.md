History of the Company
Founded in 1886 by brothers Robert Wood Johnson, James Wood Johnson, and Edward Mead Johnson, Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) began as a pioneer in sterile surgical supplies, inspired by Dr. Joseph Lister’s antiseptic techniques. The company’s first products included mass-produced sterile gauze, sutures, and wound dressings, revolutionizing surgical care by reducing infection rates. 
By the 1890s, J&J expanded into consumer health with iconic brands like JOHNSON’S® Baby Powder and, later, BAND-AID® (invented in 1921 by employee Earle Dickson). Strategic acquisitions, such as McNeil Laboratories (maker of TYLENOL®) in 1959 and Janssen Pharmaceutica in 1961, solidified its pharmaceutical dominance. Despite challenges like the 1982 TYLENOL poisoning crisis—which led to tamper-proof packaging standards—J&J maintained trust through transparency and innovation. 
Today, with over 250 subsidiaries in 60+ countries, J&J operates across pharmaceuticals (e.g., cancer drugs), medical devices (e.g., artificial joints), and consumer health, upholding its 1888 “Our Credo” commitment to patients, employees, and society.

For long-term investors, JNJ represents a "buy-and-hold forever" dividend stock, combining a rich history of shareholder returns with the resilience to thrive in evolving healthcare markets.

Here is a quick dive into Johnson & Johnson over last years
Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) demonstrated strong financial performance in Q1 2025, reporting $21.9 billion in revenue, a 4.2% year-over-year operational growth, driven by robust performance in both its Innovative Medicine and MedTech segments. 
Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) rose to $2.77, exceeding analyst expectations, while net earnings surged to $11 billion, reflecting a significant reversal of prior-year charges. 
Despite headwinds from STELARA biosimilar competition (impacting sales by ~810 basis points) and $400 million in tariff costs, the company raised its full-year operational sales guidance to $91.0–$91.8 billion, signaling confidence in sustained growth. Key drivers included:
Innovative Medicine ($13.9B, +4.2%): Strong demand for DARZALEX (multiple myeloma), TREMFYA (Crohn’s disease), and RYBREVANT (lung cancer).
MedTech ($8.0B, +4.1%): Growth in cardiovascular (Abiomed) and surgical robotics (OTTAVA trial progress).
Free cash flow reached ~$3.4 billion, supporting J&J’s 63rd consecutive annual dividend increase (now $1.30 per share) and its $55B U.S. investment plan for manufacturing and R&D. Challenges included gross margin pressure from pricing reforms and orthopedic segment declines (-3.1%), but management expects improvement in H2 2025.
