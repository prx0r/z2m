# Market Validation

## 1. Moonpig is proof that cards are a relationship/retention business

Moonpig Group FY26 (year ended 30 April 2026):
- revenue: **£373.0m**
- adjusted EBITDA: **£104.6m**
- adjusted EBITDA margin: **28.0%**
- cards revenue at Moonpig/Greetz: **£203.5m**
- attached gifting: **£123.8m**
- standalone gifting: **£8.2m**
- standard UK card price remained **£3.99**

FY25 product/retention signals:
- roughly **101m occasion reminders**
- **920k subscriptions**
- **15m creative-feature uses**
- creative-feature use up materially year over year
- AI stickers generated millions of unique images after launch

**Inference:** the value is not paper. It is remembered relationships + timely prompting + creation convenience + gift attachment.

## 2. Etsy proves personalised commerce is a major category

Etsy's 2025 annual filing says:
- Etsy marketplace GMS: **$10.5bn**
- custom/made-to-order merchandise: **about 30% of total GMS**
- gifting, holidays and personal milestones remain explicit focus occasions
- 45% of GMS was transacted through the Etsy app

This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence in the entire thesis. “Personalised” is not a novelty format.

## 3. Premium personalised stories/books show willingness to pay

Wonderbly built a global personalised-book business and was acquired by Penguin Random House in 2025. Its investor Graphite reported:
- more than **11m books sold**
- sales above **£60m**
- EBITDA above **£10m**
- expansion into adult personalisation increased AOV

**Inference:** where the product feels emotionally bespoke, the ceiling is far above a £3.99 card.

## 4. Photo products are strongly seasonal

CEWE reported 2025 group turnover of about €864.5m and EBIT of €88.2m. Its Q4 alone generated around €360.8m turnover and €86.4m EBIT.

That is the shape to expect from a Christmas-heavy physical-memory business: Q4 can dominate profit.

## 5. Legacy competitors are valuable but not invulnerable

Funky Pigeon was acquired by Card Factory in 2025 for £24m. Reported historical averages around the sale were roughly £32m revenue and £5m EBITDA.

A legacy operator being bought at that scale demonstrates:
- real demand;
- meaningful profitability;
- but not an untouchable technological moat.

## 6. Fulfilment economics are genuinely accessible to tiny entrants

Example current Prodigi starting production prices before shipping/tax:
- classic greeting card: **£1.10**
- photo print: **£0.19**
- wrapping paper: **£3.00**
- mug: **£3.64**
- gallery board: **£3.50**
- magazine: **£3.75**
- calendar: **£5.96**
- photo book: **£6.50**
- T-shirt: **£8.00**
- puzzle: **£10.00**
- aluminium ornament: **£6.00**
- cushion: **£9.00**

Not every SKU will have attractive DTC economics after shipping, payment fees, refunds, AI generation and acquisition. The important point is that **manufacturing is not the barrier**.

## 7. AI-native competitors already exist

Current small/young examples include:
- YouComics / YouStudio: upload photo → describe idea → AI gift;
- Giftimage: AI-created posters, cards, blankets, puzzles, mugs;
- Tiny Stories: consistent photo-derived characters across storybooks and gifts;
- Book4U: one-photo personalised AI hardcover;
- TinyPresent: AI preview for personalised apparel;
- Kadolia: photo + AI style + physical/digital gifting.

These validate the UX direction, but none by itself proves a durable giant business. Treat them as **behavioral validation and competitor reconnaissance**, not financial proof.

## 8. Amazon entered the primitive in June 2026

Amazon now lets U.S. shoppers describe a merch idea to Alexa for Shopping; AI creates/edit designs; Amazon produces via Merch on Demand with Prime-eligible delivery.

This changes strategy:
- generic AI merch is becoming a platform feature;
- fulfilment speed is not enough;
- the opportunity is a more specific gift problem with more context.

## 9. Q4 2026 timing is already live

Microsoft Advertising's 2026 holiday research says:
- **76%** of surveyed shoppers plan to use AI during holiday shopping;
- **63% of U.S.** and **53% of UK** shoppers plan to begin on/before Prime Day;
- value pressure remains high;
- shoppers remain open to discovering new brands.

Pinterest's July 2026 festive guidance explicitly says customers have been planning for months and warns that waiting until November means many decisions have already been made.

## Conclusion

Demand, infrastructure and technology are all validated.

The uncertain variable is **distribution**.

Therefore the MVP should be designed first as a sequence of low-cost demand experiments, not a beautiful full Moonpig replacement.
